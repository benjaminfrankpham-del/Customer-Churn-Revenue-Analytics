# Customer Churn & Revenue Analytics Assessment Report

## Executive Summary

Customer churn and revenue data was analyzed to evaluate churn behavior, customer lifetime value, revenue exposure, retention risk, and opportunities to prioritize customer retention efforts.

The analysis identified a customer base of **7,043 customers** with an overall churn rate of approximately **26.5%**. Churn is heavily concentrated among early-tenure and flexible-contract customers, with **month-to-month customers experiencing a 42.71% churn rate** and **new customers experiencing a 47.44% churn rate**.

The analysis also identified a meaningful financial exposure associated with churn risk. Approximately **$139K in revenue is classified as at risk**, with the **Medium Risk segment representing the largest exposure at $68.45K**. A retention prioritization framework identified **271 high-value, high-risk customers** as the highest-priority segment and an additional **616 customers** requiring monitoring.

Recommendations focus on reducing early-tenure churn, improving retention among month-to-month customers, increasing adoption of protective products, prioritizing high-value/high-risk customers, and aligning retention efforts with financial exposure rather than risk level alone.

## Finding 1: Customer Churn Concentration and Contract Risk

### Observation

The customer base contains **7,043 customers** with an overall churn rate of approximately **26.5%**.

Churn varies substantially by contract type. **Month-to-month customers have a 42.71% churn rate**, compared with **11.27% for one-year contracts** and only **2.83% for two-year contracts**.

The difference demonstrates a strong relationship between contract commitment and customer retention. Customers without a long-term contractual commitment represent a substantially higher churn-risk population.

### Risk Analysis

The elevated churn rate among month-to-month customers creates an ongoing revenue retention risk. Customers with flexible contracts may have fewer barriers to switching providers, making this segment more sensitive to pricing, service quality, product value, and customer experience.

The large gap between month-to-month and longer-term contracts also indicates that retention efforts should not treat the customer base as a single population. Contract structure is a meaningful segmentation variable for identifying customers who may require earlier intervention.

### Recommendation

Develop targeted retention strategies for month-to-month customers, including proactive engagement, contract conversion incentives, service-value messaging, and personalized offers.

Retention programs should focus on identifying customers before cancellation behavior occurs rather than relying primarily on reactive win-back campaigns.

## Finding 2: Early-Tenure Churn Represents the Largest Retention Risk

### Observation

Churn is highest among new customers and decreases consistently as customer tenure increases.

The observed churn rates are approximately:

- **New Customer: 47.44%**
- **Established Customer: 25.54%**
- **Loyal Customer: 16.62%**
- **Long-Term Customer: 6.61%**

New customers therefore experience a churn rate more than seven times higher than long-term customers.

### Risk Analysis

The concentration of churn among new customers suggests that the earliest stage of the customer lifecycle represents the most important retention window.

High early-tenure churn can create substantial acquisition inefficiency. Resources spent acquiring customers generate limited long-term value if customers leave before reaching established or loyal lifecycle stages.

The pattern also suggests that successful retention is associated with progressing customers from initial adoption into longer-term relationships.

### Recommendation

Establish an early-tenure retention program focused on the first stages of the customer lifecycle.

Monitor new customers for elevated churn probability and prioritize onboarding quality, product adoption, customer education, service engagement, and proactive outreach during the early customer period.

## Finding 3: Revenue Level and Security Product Adoption Are Associated With Churn Risk

### Observation

Churn varies significantly across monthly revenue levels. Customers generating **$75–$99 per month have the highest observed churn rate at 37.25%**, followed by customers generating **$100+ at 28.30%**, customers generating **$50–$74 at 26.16%**, and customers generating **under $50 at 15.74%**.

Security product adoption also shows a substantial relationship with churn. Customers without a security product have a **41.77% churn rate**, compared with **14.61% among customers with a security product**. Customers without internet service have a lower observed churn rate of **7.40%**.

### Risk Analysis

The results indicate that churn risk is not simply concentrated among the lowest-revenue customers. Customers in the $75–$99 monthly revenue range demonstrate the highest churn rate, creating an opportunity to retain customers who may already represent meaningful recurring revenue.

The security-product relationship is also important because product adoption may serve as an indicator of customer engagement, perceived value, or service attachment. Customers with broader product relationships may have stronger reasons to remain with the organization.

These relationships should be interpreted as analytical associations rather than proof that security product adoption directly causes lower churn.

### Recommendation

Prioritize retention analysis within the **$75–$99 monthly revenue segment** and evaluate the customer behaviors associated with elevated churn in this population.

Consider increasing awareness and adoption of relevant security and value-added products while monitoring whether expanded product engagement is associated with improved retention over time.

## Finding 4: Customer Value Does Not Eliminate Churn Risk

### Observation

Churn differs across customer value segments. **Low-value customers have a 31.33% churn rate**, compared with **22.49% among high-value customers**.

At the same time, high-value customers represent approximately **$11.01M in lifetime revenue**, making their retention financially important even though their average churn rate is lower.

The analysis also shows that customer lifetime value is concentrated primarily in the **$4K–$5K range**, with **2,102 customers in the $4K range** and **2,148 customers in the $5K range**.

### Risk Analysis

The lower churn rate among high-value customers should not be interpreted as low business importance. A smaller number of high-value customers can represent substantial financial exposure when their lifetime value is considered.

Conversely, the higher churn rate among low-value customers represents a larger-volume retention challenge.

This creates a strategic tradeoff between **customer risk** and **customer financial value**. Retention programs should therefore consider both dimensions rather than prioritizing customers based on churn probability alone.

### Recommendation

Use customer lifetime value alongside churn probability when determining retention priority.

High-value customers with elevated churn probability should receive targeted intervention, while scalable retention strategies should be developed for larger populations of lower-value customers with elevated churn rates.

## Finding 5: High-Value / High-Risk Customers Represent a Critical Retention Opportunity

### Observation

The retention opportunity matrix segments customers according to value and risk and identifies four distinct populations:

- **High Value / High Risk: 271 customers**
- **Low Value / High Risk: 400 customers**
- **High Value / Low Risk: 3,548 customers**
- **Low Value / Low Risk: 2,824 customers**

The **271 High Value / High Risk customers** represent the most strategically important retention segment because they combine meaningful customer value with elevated churn risk.

The broader retention prioritization framework classifies **271 customers as High Priority**, **616 as Monitor**, and **6,156 as Standard**, resulting in **887 customers** requiring elevated retention attention.

### Risk Analysis

A retention strategy based solely on churn probability could allocate significant resources toward customers who have high churn likelihood but relatively limited financial value.

The matrix demonstrates why customer value and churn risk should be evaluated together. The highest-priority population is not simply the largest risk population; it is the population where elevated risk intersects with meaningful customer value.

### Recommendation

Create a tiered retention strategy:

1. **High Priority:** Direct intervention for the 271 high-value/high-risk customers.
2. **Monitor:** Proactive monitoring and targeted engagement for customers showing elevated risk or value-related exposure.
3. **Standard:** Scalable retention and customer experience programs for the remaining customer population.

Retention resources should be allocated according to expected business impact rather than customer risk alone.

## Finding 6: Revenue Exposure Is Concentrated in the Medium-Risk Segment

### Observation

The analysis identifies approximately **$139.31K in total revenue at risk**.

Revenue exposure by risk level is:

- **High Risk: $33.53K**
- **Medium Risk: $68.45K**
- **Low Risk: $37.33K**

The Medium Risk segment therefore represents the largest revenue exposure, accounting for approximately half of total revenue at risk.

### Risk Analysis

The concentration of revenue exposure within the Medium Risk population demonstrates an important distinction between **risk severity** and **financial impact**.

High-risk customers may have the greatest probability of churn, but the largest aggregate financial exposure can occur in a broader medium-risk population.

This creates a potential blind spot if retention teams focus exclusively on the highest-risk customers. A medium-risk population can represent a substantial cumulative opportunity when the number and value of customers are considered together.

### Recommendation

Use both churn risk and financial exposure when prioritizing retention initiatives.

Maintain targeted intervention for high-risk/high-value customers while developing scalable engagement strategies for the larger Medium Risk population.

Revenue at risk should be monitored alongside customer counts and churn probability to ensure retention resources are directed toward the areas with the greatest potential financial impact.

## Finding 7: Tableau Analytics Provides a Business-Focused Retention Decision Framework

### Observation

The customer analytics platform combines customer segmentation, churn probability, customer lifetime value, revenue-at-risk analysis, risk classification, and Tableau visualization to create a business-facing retention framework.

The Tableau dashboards provide three analytical perspectives:

- **Executive Overview** — customer scale, churn, revenue, risk, and overall exposure
- **Customer Churn & Churn Analysis** — churn drivers, customer segments, and retention opportunities
- **Revenue & Customer Value** — financial concentration, customer value, revenue exposure, and tenure

The dashboards translate customer-level analytical outputs into decision-oriented views for retention and revenue management.

### Risk Analysis

Without an integrated analytical view, customer retention teams may evaluate churn, revenue, and customer value independently.

This can result in fragmented decisions where high-risk customers are prioritized without considering customer value, or where revenue exposure is evaluated without understanding the underlying churn drivers.

A business-facing dashboard creates a common analytical framework for identifying risk, quantifying financial exposure, and prioritizing action.

### Recommendation

Continue using Tableau as the business intelligence layer for customer retention analytics.

The dashboard should be used to monitor churn trends, customer value, revenue exposure, and retention priority segments while supporting deeper customer-level investigation when necessary.

As new customer data becomes available, the analytical framework should be refreshed to identify changes in churn behavior, customer value, and revenue exposure.

# Overall Recommendations

Based on the assessment findings, the customer churn and revenue analytics platform should prioritize the following actions:

1. **Prioritize early-tenure retention** by focusing intervention efforts on new customers, who demonstrate a **47.44% churn rate**.
2. **Reduce month-to-month churn** through targeted engagement and contract-conversion strategies, given the segment's **42.71% churn rate**.
3. **Prioritize the 271 high-value/high-risk customers** for direct retention intervention because they combine elevated churn risk with meaningful customer value.
4. **Monitor the broader 887 High Priority and Monitor customers** through a tiered retention strategy.
5. **Address the $68.45K Medium Risk revenue exposure** with scalable retention initiatives rather than focusing exclusively on the highest-risk population.
6. **Use customer lifetime value alongside churn probability** when allocating retention resources to balance customer risk with financial impact.
7. **Investigate product engagement relationships**, particularly the substantial churn difference associated with security-product adoption.
8. **Monitor the $75–$99 monthly revenue segment**, which has the highest observed churn rate at **37.25%**.
9. **Use Tableau dashboards as an ongoing decision-support tool** to monitor changes in churn, customer value, risk, and revenue exposure.
10. **Continuously evaluate churn model outputs and segmentation logic** to ensure retention priorities remain aligned with current customer behavior and financial objectives.

# Conclusion

The assessment demonstrates that customer churn is not evenly distributed across the customer base. The strongest retention risks are concentrated among **new customers, month-to-month customers, and customers in specific revenue and product-engagement segments**.

The analysis also demonstrates that churn probability alone is insufficient for determining retention priorities. The combination of **customer lifetime value, churn risk, revenue at risk, and customer segmentation** provides a more complete view of potential business impact.

The identification of **271 high-value/high-risk customers** provides a clear starting point for targeted retention efforts, while the **887 High Priority and Monitor customers** establish a broader population for proactive monitoring.

The **$139.31K in revenue at risk**, particularly the **$68.45K concentrated within the Medium Risk segment**, further demonstrates the importance of evaluating financial exposure alongside risk classification.

By combining Python, SQL, customer analytics, predictive churn outputs, customer lifetime value analysis, and Tableau visualization, the platform provides a foundation for data-driven retention decision-making. Continued monitoring of churn behavior, customer value, product engagement, and revenue exposure can help organizations identify emerging retention risks and allocate resources toward the customers and segments with the greatest potential business impact.
